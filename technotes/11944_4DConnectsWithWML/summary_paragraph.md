This Tech Note explains the Wireless Application Protocol (WAP) and its associated markup language, WML (Wireless Markup Language), positioning them as the standard of the day for delivering information and telephony-adjacent services to digital mobile phones and other wireless terminals. It frames WML as essential to understanding WAP itself, since WML was the content-markup language actually rendered on WAP-capable devices, analogous to how HTML relates to standard web browsers. The note's proposition is to give a general overview of WAP and WML together, followed by a specific demonstration of how 4D — via its web server/dynamic content generation capabilities — could be used to generate and serve WML content to WAP-enabled mobile devices, accompanied by a demo database illustrating the interaction between 4D and WML. Featured technology is squarely the WAP/WML stack of the early 2000s mobile-web landscape, paired with 4D's own dynamic web publishing engine repurposed to output WML markup instead of standard HTML, showing 4D's effort to extend its web capabilities into the nascent "mobile internet" space. This reflects the broader dot-com-era enthusiasm for wireless/mobile connectivity, when WAP was heavily promoted (and heavily hyped) as the future of mobile data access, well before smartphones, mobile broadband, and modern responsive web design existed. The note is a clear product-of-its-time snapshot: an ambitious but ultimately short-lived technology bet that WML would become a lasting mobile content format, illustrating how quickly emerging web/mobile standards could shift in that era. As WAP/WML use collapsed industry-wide within just a few years of this note's publication, its practical instructions have essentially no direct application to contemporary mobile development, though it remains an interesting historical artifact of 4D's early mobile-web ambitions.
