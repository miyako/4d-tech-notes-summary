This Tech Note is Part 1 of a two-part guide focused on getting more out of the 4th Dimension Form Wizard, a powerful new form design tool that debuted with 4D v6. Aimed at developers who had already encountered the basics of the Form Wizard through general V6 development work, the note goes beyond introductory usage to share practical tricks and tips for more advanced or polished form design. Part 1 specifically covers three techniques: using group boxes to visually organize objects on a form, applying styles to active objects (interactive form elements like buttons or enterable fields) for a more refined look, and adding text labels to buttons. (Part 2, per the teaser, separately covers controlling the width of alpha fields in an output form.) Throughout, the note uses the MusicPro example database — one of the sample applications bundled with a standard 4D installation — as its illustrative example, letting readers follow along with concrete, runnable form-design scenarios rather than abstract description. As a practical, UI-craftsmanship-focused note, it targets developers wanting to make their V6 forms look and behave more professionally using the still-new Form Wizard tool.
