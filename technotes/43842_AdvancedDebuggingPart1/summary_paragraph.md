This Tech Note is Part 1 of a two-part series by Josh Fletcher on debugging techniques for 4D 2004, with Part 1 covering general debugging concepts and 4D's advanced built-in debugging features, while Part 2 (a separate note) addresses an alternative text-file logging technique with an accompanying 4D component. It opens by defining debugging methodically, quoting Wikipedia's definition and stressing that debugging must be approached systematically rather than haphazardly, since disorganized attempts can obscure problems or introduce new bugs. The note lays out a seven-step debugging process: acknowledge that a bug exists, determine the pattern that reproduces it, determine its source, determine its cause, develop a fix, apply the fix, and test the fix, emphasizing that acknowledging a bug and reliably reproducing it are often the hardest and most overlooked steps. It specifically pushes back on the notion of "random" crashes, arguing that because computers are deterministic logic devices, apparently random failures always have a discoverable, reproducible cause — the real difficulty lies in isolating the pattern. The remainder of Part 1 (not fully captured in this excerpt) goes on to describe how these debugging concepts map onto concrete features available in 4D 2004's development environment, giving developers a reference for the advanced debugging tools built into the product at that time. As a foundational, methodology-focused technical note, its core process advice remains broadly applicable to software debugging in general, even though the specific 4D 2004 debugger capabilities it documents predate over a decade of subsequent 4D language, IDE, and architecture evolution (SQL engine, Project Mode, ORDA). The note is aimed at 4D developers on both Mac and Windows working with 4D 2004-era codebases.
