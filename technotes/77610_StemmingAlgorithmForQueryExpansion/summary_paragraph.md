This Tech Note covers word stemming — reducing inflected or derived words to a common root — as a technique for improving search recall in 4D applications. It walks through the Porter 2 Stemmer (also known as the Snowball English stemmer), a refinement of Martin Porter's original algorithm, and reproduces its formal definitions (suffix, vowel, doubles, R1/R2 regions, short syllable, short word) alongside the successive transformation steps (Step 0 through Step 5) and a table of exceptional word forms that must be handled specially. The note then provides a 4D component implementing this algorithm with two entry points: P2S_Stem($word) to stem a single word and P2S_Stem_Words($words) to stem a list, plus a demo database showing example input/output. The motivating use case is query expansion: for instance, stemming "assessing" to "assess" lets a search also surface "assessment" and "assessed" without the user needing to guess every inflected form, improving recall for full-text or attribute searches implemented with classic 4D QUERY/QUERY BY FORMULA commands. Because stemming is a pure text-processing algorithm with no dependency on 4D-specific data structures, this note ages unusually well compared to UI- or platform-specific Tech Notes from the same era — the linguistic algorithm is timeless, and the general approach of stemming search terms remains a standard information-retrieval technique. What has changed is that some of that value could now be delegated to full-text search engines or the more capable string-manipulation commands in modern 4D, but nothing here is deprecated.
