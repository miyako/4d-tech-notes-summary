This 2001 Tech Note by Cha Yang, a Technical Support Engineer at 4D, Inc., explains the internal workings of the Reminder Portlet in 4D Portal, which supports four reminder types -- monthly, weekly, daily, and once -- each optionally preceded by a ten-minute or five-minute advance warning before the exact due time. It first covers how each reminder type computes its due date: monthly reminders combine a stored day-of-month with the current month/year via CGI4D_ReminderChangeMonthly, rolling forward a month if the computed date/time has already passed; weekly reminders use a helper method, GEN4D_FindWeeklyDay, that loops with Add to date and Day number to find the day-count gap to the next occurrence of a chosen weekday; daily reminders simply roll to the next day if today's reminder time has passed; and "once" reminders directly store a user-specified date. The note then explains the firing logic: a Longint variable, cgi4d_l_delay, computes seconds remaining until the reminder is due by combining the date difference (multiplied by 24 hours in seconds) with the time difference, and this value is compared against 600 (ten minutes), 300 (five minutes), or 0 to decide whether to send a Ten-minute, Five-minute, or None (exact-time) reminder, tracked via a Rem_Ten_Five field that steps from "Ten" to "Five" to "None" as each tier fires. After a "None" reminder sends, GEN4D_ReassignDate advances the reminder's date according to its type (Daily +1 day, Weekly +7 days, Monthly +1 month, or marks a one-time reminder Done). Finally, the note describes how the whole system is powered by a background process, Gen4D_UtilityProcess, launched at startup via Gen4D_Startup/Shell_DoProcess, which loops with DELAY PROCESS(<>CGI4D_l_utilityProcess;3600) roughly every minute calling GEN4D_FindDue to scan and send due reminders.
