This Tech Note (TN 01-53, 4D Portal v6.7.1, Mac & Win, published November 30, 2001) presents a walkthrough of the scheduling logic behind 4D Portal's monthly/weekly/daily/once Reminder Portlet. Only a short on-page teaser paragraph survives in this archive rather than the full downloadable PDF and example database, because the original download was an old Windows self-extracting .exe installer (https://kb.4d.com/ftp://ftp.4D.com/ACI_TECHNICAL_NOTES/2001/Windows/TN_2001_50-53_(NOV)/01-53_Reminder_Portlet.exe) that could not be extracted in this environment; the original 4D Tech Notes site offered separate Windows and Mac downloads of the complete note and its companion example database, but neither is retrievable here. Based on the surviving teaser text, the note's proposition is: The Reminder Portlet offers four types of Reminders — monthly, weekly, daily and once. As the name implies, monthly reminders are sent monthly, weekly reminders are sent weekly, daily reminders are sent daily, and once reminders are sent once on the exact date and time. In addition to sending reminders at the exact time, reminders can also be set to send at ten or five minutes before a reminder is due. Setting a reminder to a ten minute reminder will send a reminder at ten minutes before a reminder is due, five minutes before a reminder is due and when the reminder is actually due. A five minute reminder sends a reminder at five minutes before a reminder is due, and when the reminder is actually due. A None reminder simply sends when the reminder is due. Its featured technology centers on 4D Portal, Reminder Portlet, Scheduled notification logic, reflecting typical concerns of the 4D 6.7.1 (4D 2000/2001 era) developer community working in binary Design Mode on classic Mac OS/early Mac OS X and Windows 98/2000/NT. This note details the scheduling logic behind 4D Portal's Reminder Portlet, covering monthly/weekly/daily/once reminder types and configurable lead-time alerts. Since 4D Portal itself was discontinued years ago, the specific portlet and its configuration options no longer exist in any supported 4D product, making this note of historical interest only, though the general reminder-scheduling logic pattern (recurring vs one-time triggers with lead-time offsets) remains a conceptually familiar problem in modern notification systems.
