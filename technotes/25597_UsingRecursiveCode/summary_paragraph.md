This Tech Note explains recursive coding — a method calling itself — and highlights a subtler, harder to detect variant: indirect recursion, where a method calls a different method which eventually calls back to the original method, sometimes through several intermediate method calls. It warns that this indirect form is often unintentional and particularly difficult to trace and maintain, and recommends against this style of programming due to those difficulties. This is general software-engineering guidance applicable to any language, illustrated here in the context of classic 4D method-based programming.
