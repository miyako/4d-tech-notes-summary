This third-party Tech Note by David Adams presents a disciplined methodology for error handling in 4D, breaking the problem into four responsibilities: Define, Catch, Record, and Analyze. It opens with a memorable "streetlight effect" anecdote to motivate why systematic error capture beats ad hoc debugging under stress, then proposes treating errors as data rather than scattered ALERT calls — defining each possible error as a record with properties like name, severity, and likely source, stored either in 4D tables or an external JSON-based structure. A central and strongly argued recommendation is to use descriptive, hierarchical error names (e.g. Account_MergeFromAccountDoesNotExist) instead of opaque numeric codes or 4D language constants, because names are self-documenting, greppable in logs, and easier to expand into an error taxonomy over time; the note explains why constants are a poor fit (31-character label limits, awkward to relocate) in favor of a lookup-table approach. It covers catching errors from three sources — failed precondition checks, errors during normal execution, and unexpected errors trapped via ON ERR CALL/GET LAST ERROR STACK — and discusses design trade-offs in recording: what should be logged, where reports should be stored, and the tension between too little detail (useless) and too much (resource-draining, unreadable logs). A worked sample implementation extends the SQLLookup demo database from TN 15-09 to show an actual logging and analysis workflow, and the note closes by suggesting scaling paths such as automatically emailing integrity reports, pushing logs to an external SQL database, or feeding a cloud-based log analytics platform for larger deployments. The conclusion frames rigorous error capture as an investment that pays for itself many times over via faster diagnosis and fewer un-caught regressions before release.
