This Tech Note introduces 4D v12's newly integrated synchronization and replication feature, which moves data-engine-level replication out of the realm of bespoke developer-built mechanisms (previously implemented with SOAP, 4D Open, or manual import/export) into a supported, built-in capability. It defines replication and synchronization conceptually, then walks through the requirements for enabling replication — connection type, language access, a primary key, enabling replication itself, and optionally "touching" existing data — followed by what happens once replication is enabled: metadata creation, new virtual fields, and replication files. It explains how replication works under the hood, including how replication metadata tracks CRUD actions (what happened), record IDs (where), and timestamps/stamps (when), illustrated through a step-by-step scenario of creating, deleting, and editing records. The note distinguishes REMOTE from LOCAL contexts (both in code and in overall software architecture), documents the REPLICATE and SYNCHRONIZE commands and their conflict-resolution behavior, and discusses real-world database-design constraints and challenges such as ID collisions, invoice numbering, and subtotal recalculation. It closes with mirror and satellite demonstration databases (including a sample "MyMeetings" application) showing the feature applied end to end, aimed at developers building distributed or offline-capable 4D solutions.
