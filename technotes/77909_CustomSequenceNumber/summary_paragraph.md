This Tech Note tackles the common requirement of tracking the order in which records or processes are completed — for example, invoice numbers or log entries — by building a custom sequence-number generator that works safely with multiple simultaneous users. It first reviews the general concept of a sequence and 4D's standard auto-increment primary key behavior, noting the potential issues that can arise (such as gaps or race conditions) with the standard approach, and introduces 4D's built-in Sequence number function as a baseline before explaining why a custom method may be preferable in some scenarios. The core technical solution relies on semaphores, explained through a real-world analogy before being applied to a concrete application example: a dedicated sequenceNumber table paired with several Table_X sample tables, and test methods (_run, _runTableX, createRecord) that exercise record creation under concurrent load. The custom Sequence Number method itself is documented in detail, covering its inputs, how it names and types its semaphore, and its function inputs/outputs, alongside the use of transactions (specifically transaction suspension) to ensure sequence values are reserved and committed correctly without collisions between simultaneous users. The note concludes by walking through how the generated sequence number flows from getNextSequenceNumber back into the createRecord method and is saved into the correct Table_X record, emphasizing that this pattern generalizes to any use case needing guaranteed, gap-free, concurrency-safe sequential numbering, such as invoicing or logging systems.
