## Overview
This Tech Note covers a troubleshooting guide to common 4D and expansion license/serial-number installation problems, including the newer diskless license scheme.

## Key Points
- It covers licensing fundamentals of the era: serial numbers, and critically, the difference between the older expansion license scheme and the newer "diskless" expansion license scheme that was being rolled out at the time.
- Its core proposition is practical and support-oriented: help any 4D user who hits a license installation problem understand why it's happening and how to resolve or work around it, rather than simply listing error codes.
- The note promises a step-by-step walkthrough of installing the new diskless expansion license on both Mac and Windows platforms, reflecting 4D's ongoing effort to modernize how license authorization was delivered (moving away from requiring a physical floppy disk or dongle-like artifact toward a more software-based diskless approach).
- As a support/FAQ-style Tech Note rather than a development technique article, its featured technology is squarely 4D's product licensing infrastructure of that specific era rather than the 4D language or application architecture.
- This kind of note would have been essential reading for 4D resellers, administrators, and IT staff managing 4D Server/4D Client deployments who needed to keep licenses valid across upgrades, machine changes, or corrupted installations.
- Because only the teaser text survives in this archive, the specific diagnostic steps and error scenarios are not preserved here, but the scope and intent of the note are clear from its introduction and stated purpose.

## Historical Context
This note documents licensing mechanics specific to the classic 4D v6.5-era product and expansion license system, including serial numbers and the transition to a "diskless" expansion license scheme on both Mac and Windows — infrastructure that has been completely replaced by 4D's modern licensing systems since. The specific troubleshooting steps are entirely obsolete since 4D's licensing architecture (activation, license files, cloud-based license management) has changed multiple times in the decades since, but the note is a useful historical record of how software licensing friction was handled in the pre-internet-activation era of desktop database software. Related updates since: 4D's licensing system has been completely replaced multiple times since 2000, including moves toward online activation and license management distinct from physical/diskless serial schemes of this era; The specific serial-number and diskless-license troubleshooting steps in this note no longer apply to any current 4D licensing mechanism.
